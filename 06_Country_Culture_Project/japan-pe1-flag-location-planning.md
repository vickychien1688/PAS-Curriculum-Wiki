## Teaching and assessment

Use pointing, circling, map/flag matching, a flag-feature comparison and bounded food/time choices. The upper-level cause question depends on explicit teacher historical explanation; it is not represented as information fully stated in the English passage. No open-ended written essays are required. Check colors, ordinary words and chunks orally before printing; scope lists are not an exhaustive measure of fluency.

The upper-level history uses a documented designated-day practice as a discussion proposal, not an approved choice of all Japanese history. Avoid asserting that everyone ate this every day, that the food was invented in wartime, or that the lunch existed solely because children disliked other foods. Keep earthquakes and volcanic eruptions distinct; detailed mechanisms belong to later PE1 pages.

## Sources and status

- [Japan flag and hinomaru bento](https://web-japan.org/factsheet/en/pdf/11NFlagAnthem.pdf): red sun circle, white rice and central red pickled plum. Other dated calendar information in this old fact sheet is not used.
- [Taiwan flag](https://english.president.gov.tw/page/96): red, white, blue and a white sun motif. Visual similarities do not establish identical symbolic meaning.
- [Taiwan CWA: seismic belts](https://scweb.cwa.gov.tw/zh-tw/guidance/faqdetail/53): Japan and Taiwan are on the Circum-Pacific belt.
- [Fukuoka City Museum](https://museum.city.fukuoka.jp/archives/leaflet/364/index02.html): designated-day schoolchild bento practice from 1939.

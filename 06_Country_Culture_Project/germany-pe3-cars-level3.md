## Vehicle and seating evidence

[Mercedes-Benz history](https://group.mercedes-benz.com/company/tradition/company-history/1885-1886.html) identifies the 1886 public outing and improved vehicle for the 1888 journey. [DPMA](https://www.dpma.de/english/our_office/publications/ingeniouswomen/berthabenz_1/index.html) identifies No.3. Model 3 variants span 1886–1894; 1888 is the journey year, not a claimed first-invention year. Omit disputed exact ages.

[Hertling’s publisher research excerpt](https://www.aisthesis.de/WebRoot/Store20/Shops/63645342/MediaGallery/leseproben/lp-9783895289415.pdf), printed p.9/PDF p.6, places Eugen at the controls, Bertha beside him, Richard in a small Rücksitz. Footnote 6 references Benz’s memoir and other sources; the research and footnote were directly read, the memoir original was not. The word alone does not establish a seat behind the rear axle; the prior handoff’s physical car-tail interpretation is withdrawn.

[Official Mercedes-Benz archive B39394](https://mercedes-benz-publicarchive.com/marsClassic/en/instance/picture/Long-distance-journey-1888.xhtml?oid=107771) was located and visually inspected. Its caption identifies a portrayal of the 1888 journey. The image shows a main-bench pair and one boy on a lower forward seat facing backward toward them. It is a retrospective portrayal, not a photograph of the trip or a dimensioned engineering record.

v4 follows this relative arrangement: Eugen and Bertha together on the main bench; Richard alone on the separate rear-facing small seat. Both journey panels share the same vehicle and positions. The previous v3 with both sons sharing the lower seat remains withdrawn. Do not invent a new car-tail seat merely to fit a translation. The historic museum empty vehicle can support its own features, not establish journey occupants. Exact trip vehicle engineering remains unproven.
